Proximity notices

How to read these notices

The bar is the gap between the two trains. The white tick is the margin: an estimate of how far the rear train needs to stop with normal braking at its current speed, plus 200 m (about 750 m at 100 km/h, 5.2 km at 300 km/h; for opposing trains, both braking distances). A bar that stops short of the tick means the gap is under that margin, which does not by itself mean anything is wrong.

The light runs faster when the gap changes faster.

Very close inside the rear train's estimated braking distance, and still closing. Close under the margin now, or will be within a minute. Watch will be within 3 minutes, or the data is uncertain. Opposing and junction notices always stay at Watch, because the data cannot confirm that two trains share a track.

Times assume both trains keep their current speed. Positions are Renfe's GPS, 15–60 s old. There is no signalling data, and the data cannot show which track a train is on, so trains that seem to be heading for each other are usually just passing on separate tracks. This is a guide, not a safety system.
